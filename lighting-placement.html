Place a Small Light for Better Low-Light Calls

A practical guide to direction, softness, glare control, and repeatable desk-light placement for video meetings.

Guide by Mara Ellison · room-first, no product-testing claim

Set the light near the viewing axis

Begin with the source near the webcam so the face is legible to the sensor. Then move it a little left or right. This small offset gives shape without creating a split-light portrait that looks severe in a small meeting tile. Treat this as a placement exercise, not an output race. The useful output is a comfortable light direction that keeps eyes visible, reflections controlled, and the fixture acceptable for the person and room that must live with it.

A placement trial has a geometric purpose. For set the light near the viewing axis, move the source, its height, or its apparent size while watching the eyes, glasses, and shadow boundary. The useful result is a comfortable face light that does not spill harshly into the rest of a shared workspace.

The placement record for set the light near the viewing axis should include where the stand, lamp, and chair belong relative to the lens. That small map turns a successful evening arrangement into a repeatable desk routine rather than a lighting trick that has to be rediscovered.

Raise the source gently

A light slightly above eye level usually avoids the under-chin shadows of a desk-level lamp. Do not raise it so far that brows create deep eye sockets. A diffuser, shade, bounce wall, or larger panel spreads the source from the speaker’s point of view. The result is usually gentler highlights and less distracting shine on skin or glasses.
